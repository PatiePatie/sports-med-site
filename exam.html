Exam Overview · 考试概览

The NPTE-PT at a Glance

The National Physical Therapy Examination is the standardized licensure exam for physical therapists in the United States. In plain terms: pass it and you are eligible to be licensed — fail it and you cannot practice. Here is everything you need to know before you book your seat.

DetailInformation
Administering bodyFSBPT — Federation of State Boards of Physical Therapy
Format225 multiple-choice questions · 5 sections × 45 questions
Scored / pretest180 scored + 45 unscored pretest items (unlabeled)
Time limit5 hours · 60 minutes per section, sections lock when advanced
Scheduled breakOne 15-minute break after the second section
Passing scoreScaled 600 on a 200–800 scale (criterion-referenced, not a fixed %)
FeesAbout $585 total — ~$485 FSBPT registration + ~$100 Prometric scheduling
Testing vendorPrometric test centers only — no remote/online option
First-time pass rateRoughly 85–90% for CAPTE-accredited DPT graduates
Attempt limits6 lifetime attempts · maximum 3 attempts in any 12 months

Data summarized from the FSBPT NPTE Candidate Handbook. Policies can change — verify current requirements at fsbpt.org before registering.

❗Three misconceptions that sink test-takers

  • The NPTE is NOT adaptive — a question gets neither easier nor harder based on your last answer. Hard questions do not mean you are failing.
  • There is NO penalty beyond a lost point for a wrong answer — answer every item, never leave a blank.
  • The 45 pretest items are interspersed and unlabeled — you cannot skip “the experimental ones.” Treat all 225 with equal care.
Format & Scoring · 形式与计分

How the Exam Actually Works

📐

Linear and fixed-form

Every candidate on a given form answers the same 225 questions. Results are comparable across forms because scores are scaled, not raw.

🔒

Sections lock

You may review and change answers within a 45-item section, but once you advance you cannot return. Budget 60 minutes per section.

⚖️

Not a percentage

The pass line is a scaled 600 set by criterion reference, so a “passing percent” does not exist. Most candidates must answer a solid majority of scored items to reach it.

🧭

Clinically reasoned

Items test reasoning across the patient/client management model — not isolated recall. Knowing a fact is not the same as applying it.

Every scored item is classified two ways: by body system and by clinical task (Examination · Foundations · Interventions). Because each live form is assembled to hit item ranges, FSBPT publishes the blueprint as ranges rather than single numbers — study to the range, not to a precise count.

📊Read the blueprint like an item writer

  • Musculoskeletal + Neuromuscular together are roughly 83–102 of the 180 scored items — about HALF the exam. Front-load these two systems.
  • Non-system points (safety, equipment, modalities, research) hide inside system scenarios — a safety item may appear inside a cardiopulmonary case. Prepare them everywhere.
  • Within every system the items span Examination → Foundations → Interventions. Learning a fact is not enough — practice applying it at the right step of the management model.
