Exam Overview · 考试概览

The USABO at a Glance

The USA Biology Olympiad is the premier biology competition for high school students in the United States. Organized by the Center for Excellence in Education (CEE), it identifies and nurtures the next generation of scientists. Here is everything you need to know.

DetailInformation
Organizing bodyCEE — Center for Excellence in Education
EligibilityU.S. citizens or permanent residents enrolled in high school (grades 9–12)
Exam roundsTwo rounds: Open Exam (Part A + Part B) → Semifinal Exam
Open Exam formatPart A: 50 multiple-choice (75 min) · Part B: 50 multiple-choice (75 min)
Semifinal format100 multiple-choice + 50 free-response (3 hours total)
Top performers~20 students advance to the Biology Olympiad Summer Study Program → 4 represent USA at IBO
TimingOpen Exam: February · Semifinal: March–April · Study Program: May–June
CostFree for the Open Exam; Semifinal requires school registration

Information summarized from the USABO official website. Policies can change — verify current requirements before registering.

🔬Why USABO matters

  • Gold/Silver/Bronze medal recognition from CEE — highly prestigious for college admissions.
  • Top 4 represent the USA at the International Biology Olympiad (IBO) — the world's premier biology competition.
  • The Summer Study Program at George Mason University is a prestigious residential science experience.

Study Strategy · 备考策略

Your Path to Medal

USABO rewards depth AND breadth. Here is a proven study strategy:

Phase 1: Foundation (3–4 months)

Build core knowledge with a comprehensive biology textbook. Campbell Biology is the gold standard. Focus on understanding concepts, not just memorizing facts.

Phase 2: Deep Dive (2–3 months)

Specialized texts for each area: Molecular Biology of the Cell (Alberts), Genetics (Pierce), Ecology (Molles). Solve past USABO Open Exams.

Phase 3: Semifinal Prep (1–2 months)

Practice free-response questions. Study IBO-style problems. Review experimental design and data analysis. Take timed practice exams.

Phase 4: Final Review (2 weeks)

Review weak areas. Practice under exam conditions. Focus on speed and accuracy. Rest well before the exam.

  • Campbell Biology (12th ed.) — the foundational text for all USABO topics.

  • Past USABO Open Exams (available at bolympiad.org) — essential for understanding question style.
  • IBO problems from previous years — the ultimate challenge for Semifinal prep.

🧬 Practice Set 1: Molecular Biology & Genetics
Molecular Biology

1. During DNA replication, which enzyme is primarily responsible for unwinding the double helix at the replication fork?

Answer: D) Helicase

Helicase unwinds the DNA double helix by breaking hydrogen bonds between base pairs. DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) relieves supercoiling ahead of the fork, while DNA polymerase synthesizes new strands and primase synthesizes RNA primers.

Genetics

2. In a cross between AaBbCc and aabbcc, what fraction of offspring would be expected to show the dominant phenotype for all three traits (assuming independent assortment)?

Answer: 1/8

For each gene: Aa × aa → 1/2 dominant phenotype. Bb × bb → 1/2 dominant phenotype. Cc × cc → 1/2 dominant phenotype. By multiplication rule: (1/2)³ = 1/8.

Biochemistry

3. Which of the following correctly describes the relationship between ΔG, ΔG°', and the actual cellular conditions for ATP hydrolysis?

Answer: The actual ΔG is more negative than ΔG°' (−30.5 kJ/mol) because cellular [ATP]/[ADP] ratios are far from equilibrium.

In cells, ATP concentration is maintained much higher than ADP, making the actual free energy release more negative (around −50 to −54 kJ/mol) than the standard free energy change.

Cell Biology

4. A cell is placed in a hypertonic solution. Which of the following will occur?

Answer: Water will leave the cell, causing it to shrink (crenation in animal cells, plasmolysis in plant cells).

In a hypertonic solution, the solute concentration outside is higher than inside. Water moves down its concentration gradient (osmosis) from inside to outside, causing cell shrinkage.

Ecology

5. In a logistic growth model (dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)), what happens to the population growth rate when N = K/2?

Answer: The population growth rate is at its maximum.

When N = K/2, the term (1 − N/K) = 0.5, and the product rN(1 − N/K) is maximized. This is the inflection point of the logistic S-curve where growth is fastest.

🔬 Practice Set 2: Advanced Topics
Molecular Biology

6. Which of the following best explains why Okazaki fragments are necessary during DNA replication?

Answer: DNA polymerase can only synthesize in the 5'→3' direction, so the lagging strand must be synthesized discontinuously.

The two strands of DNA are antiparallel. DNA polymerase reads 3'→5' and synthesizes 5'→3'. While the leading strand is continuous, the lagging strand is synthesized as short Okazaki fragments in the 5'→3' direction, which are later joined by DNA ligase.

Genetics

7. A population of 1000 organisms has the following genotype frequencies: AA = 0.36, Aa = 0.48, aa = 0.16. Is this population in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?

Answer: Yes, the population is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.

Frequency of A (p) = 0.36 + 0.48/2 = 0.6. Frequency of a (q) = 0.16 + 0.48/2 = 0.4. Expected: p² = 0.36, 2pq = 0.48, q² = 0.16. These match the observed frequencies exactly.

Plant Biology

8. In C4 plants, which anatomical feature is responsible for concentrating CO₂ around RuBisCO?

Answer: Kranz anatomy — the arrangement of bundle sheath cells (containing RuBisCO) tightly around vascular bundles, with mesophyll cells on the outside.

In C4 plants, CO₂ is initially fixed in mesophyll cells by PEP carboxylase (forming 4-carbon compounds), then transported to bundle sheath cells where it is released and refixed by RuBisCO. This spatial separation minimizes photorespiration.

Exam Tips · 考试技巧

Strategies for Success

⏱️

Time Management

Open Exam: ~90 seconds per question. Don't get stuck — flag and move on. Return to difficult questions after completing easier ones.

🧩

Process of Elimination

USABO questions often have nuanced distractors. Eliminate obviously wrong answers first, then carefully evaluate remaining options.

📝

Free-Response Writing

For Semifinal free-response: be concise but complete. Use proper terminology. Show your reasoning. Partial credit is generous.

🔬

Lab Knowledge

USABO heavily tests experimental design, data interpretation, and statistical analysis. Know your techniques: PCR, gel electrophoresis, Western blot, chromatography.
